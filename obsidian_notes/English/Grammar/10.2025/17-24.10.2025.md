# 17.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Verbs, Objects, and Complements
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
Some verbs in English require not just an **object**, but also a **complement** (a word or phrase that completes the meaning of the object).  
This complement often describes or identifies the object — telling us **what the object becomes, is, or is considered to be**.

> 🧩 Structure:  
> **Verb + Object + Complement**
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
| Structure                         | Example                          | Meaning                                                   |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Object + noun complement**      | They elected **John president.** | The complement renames or identifies the object.          |
| **Object + adjective complement** | The joke made **him angry.**     | The complement describes the object.                      |
| **Object + to-infinitive**        | She advised **me to stay.**      | The complement expresses an action related to the object. |
| **Object + -ing form**            | I caught **him stealing.**       | The complement describes the object’s ongoing action.     |
| **Object + past participle**      | We found **the door locked.**    | The complement describes the object’s state.              |
| **Object + prepositional phrase** | They regarded **him as a hero.** | The complement shows how the object is viewed.            |
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
The committee **appointed Sarah head of the department.**
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
They were trying to **inflict bad luck on** me, with their silly magic words.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _They elected John as president._  
    ✅ _They elected John president._  
    (No “as” after verbs like **appoint, elect, name, call** when used with object + noun complement.)
    
2. ❌ _I found him to be tired._  
    ✅ _I found him tired._  
    (Simpler and more natural in many cases — especially after verbs of perception like _find, see, hear_.)
    
3. Confusing **verb + object + infinitive** and **verb + object + -ing**:
    - _I saw him **cross** the street_ (entire action observed)
    - _I saw him **crossing** the street_ (action in progress)
        
4. Some verbs can take **different complements** with a change of meaning:
    - _make someone do something_ → force  
    - _make someone happy_ → cause emotion
    - _make someone a leader_ → assign a role
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **find + object + adjective**  
    → _I found the film boring._
    
2. **call + object + noun**  
    → _They called him a genius._
    
3. **appoint + object + noun**  
    → _The board appointed her director._
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Linking verbs** (be, become, seem) use complements **without** an object:  
    → _She became a doctor._
    
- **Perception verbs** (see, hear, feel, watch) often use **object + bare infinitive or -ing**:  
    → _I saw him run / running._
    
- **Causative verbs** (make, let, have, get) often follow patterns like:  
    → _make + object + verb_ / _get + object + to + verb_ / _have + object + past participle_

# 18.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Reporting People’s Words and Thoughts
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use **reported speech** (also called indirect speech) to talk about what someone said or thought without quoting them directly. When changing direct speech into reported speech, we often:
- Shift the tense one step back (backshift)
- Change pronouns and time expressions
- Use reporting verbs like: _say_, _tell_, _think_, _ask_, _wonder_, _explain_, _promise_

> Direct: _She said, “I’m tired.”_  
> Reported: _She said (that) she was tired._
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**1. Verb tense changes:**
- Present → Past
- Present Perfect → Past Perfect
- Will → Would
- Can → Could

**2. Common reporting verbs:**
- _say_, _tell_, _explain_, _ask_, _think_, _promise_, _claim_, _warn_, _admit_, _deny_
    
**3. Thought reporting often uses verbs like:**
- _think_, _believe_, _expect_, _wonder_, _hope_, _consider_

**4. No backshift (present tense kept)** when the reporting verb is in the present:
> _He says he is hungry._
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
The CEO claimed that the company **had been growing steadily**, although many employees **believed** it **was stagnating**.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
I said him, he shouldn't think only about himself with such a wonderful person who wanted to spend time together with him. 
He asked who was there.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
- ❌ _She said me that she was tired._  
    ✅ _She told me that she was tired._ / _She said that she was tired._
- ❌ Not changing time references:  
    “today” → “that day”  
    “now” → “then”  
    “yesterday” → “the day before”
- ❌ Mixing direct and indirect speech incorrectly
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **He admitted that…** – _He admitted that he had made a mistake._
2. **She promised to…** – _She promised to help me finish the project._
3. **They claimed that…** – _They claimed that the software was already tested._
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Question reporting**:  
    _He asked if I could help._ / _She wanted to know why I left early._
    
- **Imperative reporting**:  
    _He told me to stop talking._
    
- **Modal reporting**:  
    _“You must leave.” → He said I had to leave._

# 19.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Reporting Statements: _that_-clauses
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use _that_-clauses to **report what someone has said**, usually in a more formal or indirect way.
- In reported (indirect) speech, we **don’t use quotation marks**.
- The word _that_ is often **optional** in spoken English.
- The **verb tense usually shifts** one step back (known as backshifting).
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**Subject + reporting verb + (that) + clause**
- She said **(that)** she was tired.
- They explained **(that)** they had finished.

**Common Reporting Verbs:**  
say, tell, admit, deny, explain, suggest, agree, claim, warn, confirm, report, announce

**Tense Shifts (Backshifting):**
- Present → Past (He said: "I am tired." → He said **he was tired**.)
- Past → Past Perfect (She said: "I saw him." → She said **she had seen him**.)
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
The CEO confirmed **that the company would be launching a new AI division** next quarter
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
We advised him that nothing can be undone when you saved it in the cloud, so he could find a better way of placing files.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
- ❌ _He said me that…_ → ✅ _He told me that… / He said that…_
- ❌ Forgetting backshifting in exams or formal writing.
- ❌ Using direct speech with quotation marks incorrectly in indirect reporting.
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **Announce that** – The government announced **that taxes would rise**.
2. **Admit that** – He admitted **that he had lied**.
3. **Confirm that** – Scientists confirmed **that the results were accurate**.
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Reporting Questions** (_if_, _whether_, _wh-_)
- **Reporting Commands** (use of _to + verb_)
- **Reported Speech without that** (informal): _She said she was tired._
- **Conditionals and Time expressions** changes when reporting: _now → then_, _today → that day_

# 21.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">Verb + wh-clause</span>

### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
A **verb + wh-clause** structure occurs when a verb is followed by a **clause beginning with a wh-word** (who, what, where, when, why, how).  
The wh-clause acts as the **object** of the verb and expresses **indirect questions, uncertainty, or reported information.**

> 🔹 Example pattern: _verb + wh-clause_ → “I don’t know **where he lives**.”  
> The clause “where he lives” completes the verb “know.”
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**Common verbs** followed by wh-clauses:
- **ask**, **decide**, **know**, **remember**, **forget**, **explain**, **understand**, **wonder**, **see**, **tell**, **show**, **find out**

**Structure examples:**
1. **Subject + verb + wh-clause**  
    → “I don’t remember **what she said**.”
2. **Subject + verb + object + wh-clause**  
    → “He told me **where to go**.”
3. **Infinitive + wh-word** (especially with _decide, know, remember, learn_)  
    → “I don’t know **what to do**.”
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
She couldn’t explain **why her hands were trembling**, but everyone could sense the truth she was hiding.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
They were trying to explain me how to updated that system, but I wasn't insightful whether or not I'm able to fulfil it on my own next time. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I don’t know where does he live._  
    ✅ _I don’t know where he lives._  
    → (No inversion in indirect questions!)
    
2. ❌ _Tell me what should I do._  
    ✅ _Tell me what I should do._
    
3. Forgetting to use **“to”** with infinitive forms:  
    ❌ _I don’t know what do._  
    ✅ _I don’t know what to do._
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **find out what happened** — “Detectives are still trying to find out what happened that night.”
2. **decide where to go** — “Let’s decide where to go before it gets dark.”
3. **understand how it works** — “Once you understand how it works, it becomes simple.”
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Indirect (embedded) questions** → _Can you tell me where she went?_
- **Infinitive wh-clauses** → _I don’t know what to say._
- **Reported speech with wh-clauses** → _He asked why I was late._
- **Noun clauses** (that-clauses vs. wh-clauses) → _I know that he’s coming / I know when he’s coming._

# 22.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Modal Verbs in Reporting
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
When reporting speech that contains **modal verbs** (can, may, must, etc.), some of them **change their form** in reported speech, while others **remain the same**.  
The change depends on **tense shifting** and on whether the modal verb has a **past equivalent**.
🔹 Direct speech → Reported speech  
“I can help you.” → He said he **could** help me.

“You must finish it.” → She said I **had to** finish it.
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
|**Modal (Direct)**|**Reported Form**|**Notes / Meaning**|
|---|---|---|
|can|could|ability / possibility|
|may|might|permission / possibility|
|must|had to|obligation (past reference)|
|shall|should|future / suggestion|
|will|would|future intention / promise|
|could|could|stays the same|
|might|might|stays the same|
|should|should|stays the same|
|would|would|stays the same|
|ought to|ought to|no change|
|need to|needed to|sometimes changes|
|must (deduction)|must|often unchanged for logical deduction|
**Example pattern:**

> Subject + reporting verb (say/tell/claim/explain) + (that) + subject + modal verb (changed or unchanged)
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
“You may not survive without oxygen,” the instructor warned.  
→ The instructor warned that we **might not survive** without oxygen.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
"You may find a better way if you spend more time for deciding." - he said.
He said that you might find a bettwe way if you spend more time for deciding. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _He said I can help him._  
    ✅ _He said I **could** help him._  
    → (Wrong tense – “can” becomes “could”)
    
2. ❌ _She told me I must come early yesterday._  
    ✅ _She told me I **had to** come early yesterday._  
    → (“must” for obligation changes to “had to”)
    
3. ❌ _He said we may finish soon._  
    ✅ _He said we **might** finish soon._
    

⚠️ **Exception:** When the reporting verb is in the **present**, modals don’t change:

> She says she **can** swim. (still true)
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **said he could** – “He said he could fix the issue himself.”
2. **explained that we would** – “She explained that we would need more time.”
3. **told me I had to** – “My doctor told me I had to rest for a week.”
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Reported Speech (Tense Shifts)** – general rules for changing verb tenses in reported speech.
- **Modal verbs of deduction** – _must_, _might_, _can’t_ often stay the same because they express logical meaning.
- **Reporting commands and requests** – using _told_, _asked_, _ordered_ + infinitive (e.g. _He told me to leave._).

# 24.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">A, An, The, and Zero Article</span>

### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
Articles are small words placed before nouns to show **whether the noun is specific or general**.
- **A / An** → used when mentioning something for the first time or something non-specific.
- **The** → used for specific things, already known or unique.
- **Zero article (∅)** → used with general or uncountable ideas, plural nouns, and many proper names.

> 🔹 _A_ is used before consonant sounds.  
> 🔹 _An_ is used before vowel sounds.  
> 🔹 _The_ is used before both singular and plural nouns when referring to something specific.  
> 🔹 Zero article means no article is used.
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
| **Type**                        | **Used Before**                                     | **Example**                                                      |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A / An**                      | Singular countable nouns (first mention, general)   | _I saw **a** cat._ / _She ate **an** apple._                     |
| **The**                         | Specific noun (already mentioned, known, unique)    | _I fed **the** cat again._ / _The sun is bright today._          |
| **Zero article (∅)**            | Plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally | _Cats are cute._ / _Milk is healthy._                            |
| **The + adjective**             | Refers to a group of people                         | _The rich should help the poor._                                 |
| **A / An + adjective + noun**   | Used when introducing or describing something new   | _He’s **an interesting person.**_                                |
| **The + superlative / ordinal** | When describing something unique or best            | _She is **the best** student._ / _I live on **the first**floor._ |
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
After spending a week in **the** mountains, I realized that **a** simple life without **the** noise of **the** city brings **∅** peace that no luxury can offer.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
If you want to be a hero in our not simple world. First you should realize the path given to you and people who will come along with you.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _She is teacher._ → ✅ _She is **a** teacher._
    
2. ❌ _I like the music._ (when speaking generally) → ✅ _I like **music**._
    
3. ❌ _The Mount Everest is high._ → ✅ _∅ Mount Everest is high._
    
4. ❌ _He’s an university student._ → ✅ _He’s **a** university student._ (because “university” starts with a consonant sound /juː/)
    
5. ❌ _She has a long hair._ → ✅ _She has **long hair.**_
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **In the morning / in the afternoon / at night**
2. **Go to the cinema / the theatre / the gym**
3. **Have a look / take a break / make an effort**
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Demonstratives:** _this, that, these, those_ (used instead of articles when specifying).
- **Possessives:** _my, your, his, her, their_ (replace articles).
- **Quantifiers:** _some, any, few, many_ (often used with zero article nouns).

# 25.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">A / An, The, and Zero Article (3)</span>

### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use **zero article (∅)** with plural and uncountable nouns when speaking **in general** — about all examples of something, not specific ones.  
We use **the** when referring to something **specific**, known, or previously mentioned.  
We use **a / an** when introducing something **for the first time** or when it’s **one of many possible examples**.

> 🔹 _Zero article:_ “Dogs are loyal animals.” (= dogs in general)  
> 🔹 _The:_ “The dogs next door are noisy.” (= specific dogs)  
> 🔹 _A / An:_ “I saw a dog in the park.” (= one, not known which)
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**1️⃣ Zero article with plural / uncountable nouns:**
- “Children love chocolate.” (= all children, not specific ones)
- “Time is precious.” (= the idea of time generally)

**2️⃣ Zero article with holidays, seasons, days, months:**
- “We met on Monday.” (= general day name)
- “I’ll see you on the Friday after the concert.” (= specific Friday)
- “Winter is my favourite season.” / “In the summer of 2018 we travelled to Iceland.”

**3️⃣ With times of day and night:**
- “Let’s finish by noon.”
- “He woke up before dawn.”
- “The evening was calm and quiet.” (= referring to a particular evening)

**4️⃣ With means of transport and communication (by + zero article):**
- “She goes to work by bus.”
- “We travelled by train across the country.”
- “I contacted him by email.”

**5️⃣ With general periods or unique occasions, use _It was a / an … + modifying phrase_:**
- “That was a summer I’ll always remember.” (= compared to other summers)
- “It was an evening filled with laughter.”
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
In **the spring of 2022**, I travelled through **Europe by train**, waking early before **dawn** and working remotely during **the evenings**.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
Summer is my the most unloved season, tbh I abhor hot days. But today the summer's evening was incredibly peasant with it's temperature.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I love the music._ (when talking about music in general)  
    ✅ _I love music._
    
2. ❌ _The Christmas is my favourite holiday._  
    ✅ _Christmas is my favourite holiday._
    
3. ❌ _She goes to work by the bus._  
    ✅ _She goes to work by bus._
    
4. ❌ _I’ll see you in next Monday._  
    ✅ _I’ll see you on Monday._
    
5. ❌ _The midday was very hot._  
    ✅ _Midday was very hot._
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **By car / by bus / by plane**
2. **In the morning / at night / by noon**
3. **In summer / in the spring of + year**
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Zero article with institutions:** “He’s at school / at university / in prison.”
- **Definite article with unique nouns:** “The sun, the moon, the Internet.”
- **Indefinite article with descriptions:** “She’s a teacher.”
