# 02.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
**Present Perfect (I have done)**
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule (Translate to Russian if needed)</span>
Present Perfect is used to describe actions or experiences that are connected to the present. The exact time of the action is not important. Instead, the focus is on the result, experience, or effect in the present moment.
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
- **Affirmative:** Subject + have/has + past participle  
    _I have finished my work._
- **Negative:** Subject + haven’t/hasn’t + past participle  
    _She hasn’t called me back._
- **Question:** Have/Has + subject + past participle?  
    _Have you ever been to London?_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
“Scientists have recently discovered evidence that completely changes our understanding of human history.”
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
I've never tried that way or tool, but it seems that it's worth trying and I'll do it this weekend.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
❌ _I have seen him yesterday._  
✅ _I saw him yesterday._ (specific past time → use Past Simple)

❌ _She don’t has finished her essay._  
✅ _She hasn’t finished her essay._
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
- **Have you ever…?** → _Have you ever tried surfing?_
- **It’s the first/second time…** → _It’s the first time I’ve spoken in public._
- **I’ve just / already / yet** → _I’ve just arrived. She has already left. Have you finished yet?_
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Past Simple** → used for actions with a specific past time reference.
- **Present Perfect Continuous** → used to emphasize duration (I have been studying).
- **Past Perfect** → used for actions completed before another past action (I had done).

# 03.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Present Perfect Continuous (I have been doing)
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use the Present Perfect Continuous to emphasize the **duration** or **continuity** of an action that started in the past and is still happening now, or has very recently stopped but has a visible effect/result in the present.
- Focus on **“how long”** something has been happening.
- Focus on **unfinished or repeated actions**.  
    ❌ Not used with stative verbs (e.g. _know, believe, like_).
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
- **Affirmative:** Subject + have/has + been + verb(-ing)  
    _She has been studying all day._
- **Negative:** Subject + haven’t/hasn’t + been + verb(-ing)  
    _They haven’t been working lately._
- **Question:** Have/Has + subject + been + verb(-ing)?  
    _Have you been waiting long?_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
“By the time the manager arrived, the team had been discussing the problem for over an hour without finding a solution.”
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
Today I've been doing nothing useful until now, after this moment i'm trying to accomplish something meaningful for today. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
❌ _I am learning English for five years._  
✅ _I have been learning English for five years._

❌ _She has been knowing him since childhood._  
✅ _She has known him since childhood._ (stative verb → Present Perfect Simple)
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
- **Have you been …ing?** → _Have you been working out lately?_
- **I’ve been meaning to…** → _I’ve been meaning to call you._
- **It has been raining…** → very common in weather talk: _It has been raining all day._
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Present Perfect Simple (I have done)** → focuses on results (_I’ve read the book_).
- **Past Perfect Continuous (I had been doing)** → emphasizes duration before a past moment.
- **Present Continuous (I am doing)** → focuses on action happening right now.

# 04.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Present Perfect Simple vs. Present Perfect Continuous
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
- **Present Perfect Simple (have/has + past participle)** → focuses on **result, completion, or quantity**.
    > _What has been achieved or happened up to now._
- **Present Perfect Continuous (have/has + been + -ing form)** → focuses on **duration, activity, or continuity**.
    > _How long something has been happening or what activity has been in progress._

🧠 **In short:**
- Use **Simple** → for _finished results_ or _number of times._
- Use **Continuous** → for _unfinished actions_ or _visible effects of ongoing activity._
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**✅ Present Perfect Simple:**  
`Subject + have/has + past participle`

> I have finished my work.  
> She has written three books.

**✅ Present Perfect Continuous:**  
`Subject + have/has + been + verb-ing`

> I have been working all day.  
> He has been waiting for you since morning.

**⏱ Common Time Expressions:**
- _for_, _since_, _lately_, _recently_, _ever_, _never_, _so far_, _already_, _yet_, _just_, _how long_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
“I’ve been writing code for six hours, and I’ve fixed twelve bugs so far — progress finally feels tangible.”
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
Today I've been trying to live my lone life extraordinary with meaningful aspirations.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I am learning English for two years._  
    ✅ _I have been learning English for two years._  
    → Don’t use **Present Continuous** with “for/since” when talking about time up to now.
    
2. ❌ _I have been finishing my report._  
    ✅ _I have finished my report._  
    → Don’t use **Continuous** with **completed actions**.
    
3. ❌ _He has been reading ten books this month._  
    ✅ _He has read ten books this month._  
    → Use **Simple** when focusing on **number or quantity**.
    
4. Remember: some **state verbs** (know, like, believe, understand) are **not used in continuous** forms.
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **Have been waiting** → I’ve been waiting for ages!
2. **Have lived / Have been living** → I’ve lived here all my life. / I’ve been living here for years.
3. **Have worked / Have been working** → He has worked at Google since 2018. / He has been working there since 2018.

_(Both can be correct — Continuous emphasizes process; Simple emphasizes permanence.)_
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Past Perfect Simple / Continuous** → connects two past moments (e.g. _I had been studying before the exam started._)
- **Present Simple / Continuous** → for habitual or ongoing present actions, but without connection to the past.
- **Future Perfect** → for actions that will be completed before a future time (e.g. _I will have finished by tomorrow._)

# 05.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
present perfect and past simple
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
Both tenses talk about **past actions**, but they focus on **different connections with the present**:
- **Present Perfect (have/has + past participle)** → connects the past with the **present moment**
    > Focus: **result or relevance now**.  
    > Time is **not finished** (today, this week, recently).
- **Past Simple (verb + -ed / 2nd form)** → describes an action that happened at a **specific time in the past**.
    > Focus: **completed action**.  
    > Time is **finished** (yesterday, last year, in 2020).

🧠 **In short:**
- **Present Perfect** → _now still matters._
- **Past Simple** → _completely finished, past world._
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**✅ Present Perfect:**  
`Subject + have/has + past participle`

> I have seen this movie before.  
> She has just arrived.

**✅ Past Simple:**  
`Subject + verb (2nd form or -ed)`

> I saw this movie yesterday.  
> She arrived an hour ago.

**⏱ Common Time Markers:**
- **Present Perfect:** ever, never, just, already, yet, recently, lately, so far, today, this week, since, for
- **Past Simple:** yesterday, last week, two days ago, in 2010, when I was a child
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
“I’ve visited Japan twice, but the last time I went there, I finally climbed Mount Fuji.”  
_(Perfect = total experience; Past Simple = one finished event.)_
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
I've been able to write her the whole day until now, but I decided not to do it. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I have seen him yesterday._  
    ✅ _I saw him yesterday._  
    → Don’t use **Present Perfect** with **finished time expressions** (_yesterday, last year, ago_).
    
2. ❌ _Did you ever be in London?_  
    ✅ _Have you ever been to London?_  
    → Use **Present Perfect** with _ever/never/lately_.
    
3. ❌ _I have lost my keys yesterday._  
    ✅ _I lost my keys yesterday._ or _I have lost my keys (and I can’t find them now)._  
    → Choose based on whether the result affects **now**.
    
4. ❌ Mixing both without logic:  
    _I have been to Paris last year._ → ✗  
    _I went to Paris last year._ → ✓  
    _I have been to Paris many times._ → ✓
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **Have you ever been...** → _Have you ever been abroad?_
2. **Have just done...** → _I’ve just finished my homework._
3. **Did you... yet?** (American informal mix) → _Did you eat yet?_
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Present Perfect Continuous** → emphasizes _duration or process_ (e.g. _I’ve been studying for two hours._)
- **Past Perfect** → for actions _before another past moment_ (e.g. _I had finished before he arrived._)
- **Past Continuous** → for _background actions_ (e.g. _I was reading when the phone rang._)

# 06.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
**Past Perfect**
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use the **past perfect** to talk about an action that happened **before another action in the past**.  
It shows **sequence** and **cause**: one thing was completed **before** something else happened.  
→ It’s formed with **had + past participle**.

🧠 _Think of it as “the past before the past.”_
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
✅ **Affirmative:**  
Subject + _had_ + past participle
> She **had finished** the report before the meeting started.

✅ **Negative:**  
Subject + _had not (hadn’t)_ + past participle
> They **hadn’t seen** the film before yesterday.

✅ **Question:**  
_Had_ + subject + past participle?
> **Had** you **ever tried** sushi before that trip?

⏳ **Time markers:** _before, after, by the time, already, until, when, just, never_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
By the time the storm reached the coast, most of the villagers **had already evacuated**, leaving behind a ghostly silence that felt heavier than the rain itself.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
Before he came to us, we had already left that city. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
⚠️ **1. Overusing the past perfect:**  
Don’t use it for _every_ past action—only when there’s a clear “before another past event.”

⚠️ **2. Forgetting the second past event:**

> ❌ I had eaten breakfast. (incomplete idea)  
> ✅ I had eaten breakfast **before I left** for work.

⚠️ **3. Confusing it with the past simple:**

> ❌ When I arrived, he left.  
> ✅ When I arrived, he **had left**. (He left first!)
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **had already done** — “She had already packed when I called.”
2. **had never seen** — “I had never seen such courage before that day.”
3. **had just finished** — “He had just finished his coffee when the phone rang.”
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Past Simple:** for events that happened in sequence (no overlap).
- **Past Perfect Continuous:** to show _duration_ before another past action (_He had been studying for hours before the test_).
- **Present Perfect:** connects past with present (_I have seen it before_).

## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
**Future Perfect & Future Continuous (Simple Continuous)**
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
- **Future Perfect** describes an action that **will be completed before a specific time in the future**.  
    🔹 _By this time tomorrow, I will have finished the report._
    
- **Future Continuous** describes an action that **will be in progress at a specific time in the future**.  
    🔹 _At 8 p.m., I will be working on my project._
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**Future Perfect:**  
✅ will + have + past participle (V3)  
→ _I will have done / she will have arrived / they will have finished_

**Future Continuous:**  
✅ will + be + verb-ing  
→ _I will be working / he will be sleeping / they will be traveling_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
_By the time the sun rises over the city, the hackers will have already breached the system, and the authorities will be scrambling to trace their signal._ 🌃💻
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
Tomorrow By the morning, I hope that I will have already done with my all study lessons.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I will have working at 6._ → ✅ _I will be working at 6._ (Use continuous for actions in progress)
    
2. ❌ _I will be finish by then._ → ✅ _I will have finished by then._ (Use perfect for completed actions)
    
3. Mixing time markers:
    - Future Perfect → _by_, _before_, _by the time_
    - Future Continuous → _at_, _around_, _this time tomorrow_
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **will have finished / will have completed** – used with deadlines
2. **will be waiting / will be working** – describing expected ongoing activities
3. **by the time + clause** – common time marker for future perfect
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Present Perfect:** connection to the present
- **Past Perfect:** completed before another past action
- **Future Simple:** simple statement about the future (no progress or completion nuance)

# 08.10.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">Can, Could, Be Able To & Be Allowed</span>

### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
We use **can**, **could**, **be able to**, and **be allowed to** to talk about **ability**, **possibility**, and **permission**, but they differ in **time** and **formality**.

- **Can / Could** → Modal verbs for **general ability** and **permission**.
- **Be able to** → Alternative to “can” when we need other tenses or more precision.
- **Be allowed to** → Focuses only on **permission**, not ability.
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
|Tense|Ability|Permission|
|---|---|---|
|Present|can / am–is–are able to|can / am–is–are allowed to|
|Past|could / was–were able to|could (rare, for general) / was–were allowed to|
|Future|will be able to|will be allowed to|
|Perfect|have/has been able to|have/has been allowed to|
|Continuous|being able to (rare, formal)|being allowed to (possible in formal English)|
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
Although the storm was fierce, the rescue team **was able to** reach the stranded hikers before nightfall — an achievement few **could** have imagined.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
I was allowed to change permissions for that component in the code, but after changing rights, no one still was able to change it.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _I will can do it tomorrow._ → ✅ _I will be able to do it tomorrow._
2. ❌ _I was allowed to swim fast._ (means permission, not ability) → ✅ _I was able to swim fast._
3. ❌ _Could you go to the party yesterday?_ (not successful action) → ✅ _Were you able to go to the party yesterday?_
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **Can’t help doing** → “I can’t help laughing.”
2. **Be able to cope / handle / adapt** → “He was able to handle the crisis calmly.”
3. **Be allowed to enter / stay / leave** → “Only staff are allowed to enter this area.”
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Be capable of + ing** → “She’s capable of solving complex problems.”
- **Manage to + V** → Similar to “was able to” in past success.
- **Be permitted to** → Formal version of “be allowed to.”
