# 21.11.2025
## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
no, none (of), not any
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
All three forms — **no**, **none of**, and **not any** — express **zero quantity or absence** of something, but they differ in grammar structure and emphasis.
- **no + noun** → used before a noun, without _a/an_ or _any_.
- **none (of)** → used as a pronoun, often before _the_, _my_, _this_, or plural nouns/pronouns.
- **not any** → used after a verb to make the sentence negative; slightly more formal or emphatic than _no_.
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
|Form|Structure|Example|Usage|
|---|---|---|---|
|**no + noun**|no + singular/plural noun|_There is **no milk** left._|general negative meaning; simple and direct|
|**none (of)**|none of + determiner/noun/pronoun|_**None of my friends**_ came to the party.*|used when referring to a specific group|
|**not any + noun**|not + any + noun|_There isn’t **any reason** to worry._|used for emphasis or in formal tone; same meaning as _no_|
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
**No evidence** was found at the scene, and **none of the witnesses** remembered seeing **any** suspicious person nearby.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
I have no clue to describe why no one decided to take part in our play.
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. ❌ _There isn’t no milk left._ → ✅ _There isn’t **any** milk left._  
    _(Avoid double negatives.)_
    
2. ❌ _None people arrived._ → ✅ _**None of the people**_ arrived.*  
    _(“None” always needs “of” before a determiner.)_
    
3. ❌ _I don’t have no idea._ → ✅ _I don’t have **any idea** / I have **no idea.**_  
    _(Choose one negative form only.)_
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **no doubt** – “There’s no doubt he’ll succeed.”
2. **none of your business** – “That’s none of your business.”
3. **no reason / no chance / no problem** – “There’s no chance of failure.”
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **nothing / nobody / nowhere** – other negative pronouns expressing zero quantity or presence.
- **any / some** – positive or interrogative counterparts.
- **neither / either** – used when referring to two choices.

## <span style="color: LightBlue;">GRAMMAR TOPIC</span>
Relative pronouns
### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
Relative pronouns connect a noun to a clause that gives **extra information** about it.  
They function as **subjects, objects, or possessives** inside the relative clause.

Common relative pronouns:
- **who** → people
- **whom** → people (formal object)
- **whose** → possession (people or things)
- **which** → things & animals
- **that** → people, things (informal; used in defining clauses)
    
Two clause types:
- **Defining** → essential information; no commas
- **Non-defining** → extra/additional information; commas required
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
1. **who / that** + verb
> The man **who lives next door** is a writer.

2. **whom / who / that** + subjec
> The woman **whom you met yesterday** is my mentor.

3. **whose** + noun
> The student **whose laptop broke** borrowed mine.

4. **which / that** + clause
> The laptop **that I bought last year** still works perfectly.

5. **Non-defining:** , who / which ,
> His brother, **who works abroad**, is visiting next week.

6. **Omitting the pronoun** (object only)
> The book (that) I ordered arrived today.
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
The neuroscientist **whose theories transformed modern cognition studies** recently published a paper **which challenges long-held assumptions in the field**.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
My new friend that I made a couple of months before showed me his real face. 
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. **Using “which” for people**  
    ❌ The person _which_ called me  
    ✅ The person **who** called me
    
2. **Forgetting commas in non-defining clauses**  
    ❌ My boss who lives in Italy sent an email  
    ✅ My boss, **who lives in Italy**, sent an email
    
3. **Using “whose” only for people**  
    “Whose” works for things too:  
    → _the company **whose** values I admire_
    
4. **Using “that” in non-defining clauses**  
    ❌ My brother, **that** lives in Spain…  
    ✅ My brother, **who** lives in Spain…
    
5. **Incorrect omission**  
    ❌ The person helped me  
    (missing object)  
    ✅ The person **(who/whom) I helped**
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **the man who… / the woman who… / the person who…**
2. **the thing that… / the reason why… / the place where…**
3. **the book which… / the idea which…**
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Relative adverbs:** _where, when, why_
- **Omission in relative clauses:** _(that) I bought…_
- **Participial clauses:** _The man walking down the street…_ (alternative to a relative clause)
- **Appositive relative clauses** (advanced): _The idea, which seemed impossible at first, succeeded._

## <span style="color: LightBlue;">Adjective + to-infinitive, –ing, that-clause, wh-clause</span>

### <span style="color: Green;">Rule</span>
Many adjectives can be followed by different clause structures.  
Each structure changes the meaning slightly and is used in different communication contexts:

- **Adjective + to-infinitive** → describes feelings, willingness, difficulty, ease, readiness
- **Adjective + –ing clause** → describes something causing the feeling (active meaning)
- **Adjective + that-clause** → expresses opinion, evaluation, certainty, surprise
- **Adjective + wh-clause** → shows uncertainty, lack of information, or decisions
### <span style="color: Yellow;">Forms & Structures</span>
**1) Adjective + to-infinitive**
- useful for expressing _emotions, willingness, effort, or judgement_  
    Structure: **adj + to + verb**  
    Examples: _easy to understand, ready to start, afraid to ask_
    

**2) Adjective + –ing clause**
- used when the noun/adjective is affected by an ongoing or general situation  
    Structure: **adj + –ing clause**  
    Examples: _busy doing something, worth seeing, difficult living in…_
    

**3) Adjective + that-clause**
- used for opinions, beliefs, reactions, certainty  
    Structure: **adj + that + clause**  
    Examples: _sure that…, surprised that…, happy that…_
    

**4) Adjective + wh-clause**
- used when you don’t know or must decide something  
    Structure: **adj + wh-word + clause**  
    Examples: _unsure where…, confused why…, curious how…_
### <span style="color: Orange;">1 High-level Example</span>
The team was **uncertain whether to continue** the experiment, **aware that the results were inconsistent**, and **curious why the data behaved** in such unpredictable ways.
### <span style="color: Grey;">My Personal Example</span>
It's not disrespectful to say that your kind of people worth nothing after what you've done.  
### <span style="color: Blue;">Common Mistakes & Traps🌓</span>
1. **Using that-clauses after adjectives that require to-infinitive**  
    ❌ _I'm ready that I start._  
    ✅ _I'm ready to start._
    
2. **Using wh-clauses when a to-infinitive is needed**  
    ❌ _I’m not sure how to fix it or not._  
    ✅ _I’m not sure whether to fix it or not._
    
3. **Mixing –ing clauses incorrectly**  
    ❌ _It’s difficult living solve this issue._  
    ❌ _It’s difficult to living here._  
    ✅ _It’s difficult living here._ / _It’s difficult to solve this issue._
    
4. **Forgetting that many adjectives require “that”**  
    ❌ _I'm happy you came._  
    (informal ok, but grammar prefers)  
    ✅ _I'm happy that you came._
### <span style="color: Purple;">3 Famous Uses / Collocations</span>
1. **likely to / unlikely to**  
    _He’s likely to succeed._
    
2. **afraid that / afraid to**  
    _I’m afraid that he’s right._  
    _I’m afraid to ask._
    
3. **sure that / unsure what**  
    _I’m sure that he’ll call._  
    _I’m unsure what to choose._
### <span style="color: Red;">Other Related Grammar Forms</span>
- **Noun + to-infinitive** → _a decision to move, a plan to expand_
- **It + adjective + that-clause** → _It’s essential that you attend…_
- **Extraposition “It is…” structures** → _It is surprising how… / It is unclear why…_
- **Adjective + preposition + –ing** → _good at solving, interested in learning_
